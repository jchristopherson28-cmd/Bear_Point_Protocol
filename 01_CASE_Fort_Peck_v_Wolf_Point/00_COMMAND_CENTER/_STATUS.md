## IMMEDIATE SITUATION

> [!danger] CASE SUMMARY
> **Complainant:** Fort Peck Tribal Executive Board (Assiniboine & Sioux Tribes)
> **Victims:** Native American students in Wolf Point School District (majority of student body)
> **Defendant:** Wolf Point School District (Roosevelt County, Montana)
> **Incident date:** June 28, 2017 (Title VI complaint filed with DOE OCR + DOJ)
> **Summary:** Systemic discrimination against Native students in a majority-Native school district controlled by white minority. 46-page tribal complaint documents disparate discipline, denial of academic opportunities, discriminatory hiring, cultural hostility, and institutional contribution to student self-harm and suicide. DOE confirmed discrimination in 2003, monitored 2003–2008, but patterns persisted. ProPublica/NYT investigation published Dec 28, 2018 triggered OCR investigation opening same day. ACLU of Montana co-counsel throughout.

> [!warning] PRIOR FEDERAL MONITORING (2003–2008)
> DOE affirmed instances of discrimination against Native students in 2003 following a three-day community forum sponsored by Fort Peck education department. District monitored for five years. Discrimination persisted after monitoring ended — same patterns, different personnel.

> [!danger] STUDENT DEATHS
> **Jayden Joe** — Wolf Point High School junior, died by suicide during school hours in March 2017, three months before tribal complaint filed. Principal had berated him for poor attendance, per two student witnesses. Second student at the high school in seven years to die after being disciplined by administrators. Roxanne Gourneau's son also died by suicide after school discipline.

> [!success] OCR INVESTIGATION OPENED DEC 28, 2018
> Letter sent hours after ProPublica/NYT publication. Investigation to focus on: disparate discipline, remedial program tracking, denial of AP access, inadequate special education services, discriminatory hiring, culturally hostile environment. OCR investigators visited Wolf Point April 2019 to interview students and parents.

## CRITICAL EVENTS

### Jun 28, 2017 — TITLE VI COMPLAINT FILED
- Fort Peck Tribal Executive Board filed 46-page complaint with DOE OCR and DOJ simultaneously
- First time tribes filed complaint with both federal agencies on children's behalf based on school district policy
- Attorney: Melina Healey (Equal Justice Works fellow)
- ACLU of Montana signed on in support
- Allegations: disparate discipline, denial of academic opportunities, discriminatory hiring, cultural hostility, contribution to student self-harm/suicide
- 180-day response window for federal agencies

### Mar 2017 — JAYDEN JOE SUICIDE
- Wolf Point High School junior died by suicide during school hours
- Principal had berated him for poor attendance (per two student witnesses)
- Second student at the school in seven years to die after administrator discipline
- Three months before tribal complaint filed
- Borge Park, Wolf Point — location of death

### Dec 28, 2018 — PROPUBLICA/NYT INVESTIGATION PUBLISHED
- Joint investigation by Annie Waldman (ProPublica) and Erica L. Green (NYT)
- Photographer: Annie Flanagan
- Profiled Ruth Fourstar (17, honor roll student pushed to remedial)
- Documented: Native students 2x+ suspension rate, white students 14.6x more likely to take AP courses
- 94% of Native students below reading proficiency vs 49% white
- Nearly 1/5 of Native HS students attempted suicide in past year (MT education survey 2017)
- Same day: OCR letter announced investigation

### Aug 2013 — VOTING RIGHTS LAWSUIT
- Jackson v. Wolf Point School District filed in U.S. District Court
- ACLU of Montana + ACLU National Voting Rights Project
- 7 Native American plaintiffs
- White voting district: 1 board member per 143 residents
- Native voting district: 1 board member per 841 residents (5.9x disparity)
- District conceded malapportionment Jan 2014
- Settlement: 5 single-member districts + 1 at-large (Apr 2014)

### 2003–2008 — FIRST FEDERAL MONITORING
- DOE affirmed discrimination against Native students (2003)
- Triggered by three-day community forum sponsored by Fort Peck education department
- District monitored for five years
- Discrimination persisted after monitoring ended
