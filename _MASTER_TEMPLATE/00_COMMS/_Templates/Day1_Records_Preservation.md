## I. RECORDS TO BE PRESERVED

You are hereby directed to immediately identify, locate, and preserve all documents, data, and electronically stored information (ESI) in the possession, custody, or control of {SCHOOL_DISTRICT_NAME}, its officers, employees, agents, and contractors relating to any of the following:

**Student Records:**
- All education records as defined under FERPA (20 U.S.C. § 1232g), including but not limited to: academic records, attendance records, behavioral and disciplinary records, special education and 504 records, health records, and counseling records relating to {VICTIM_NAME}
- All student information system entries, including any modifications or deletions made at any time to records relating to {VICTIM_NAME}
- All internal notes, memoranda, emails, text messages, or other communications among district staff discussing {VICTIM_NAME} or this family

**Communications:**
- All correspondence (email, letter, text, voicemail) between any district employee and myself, {COMPLAINANT_NAME}, regarding {VICTIM_NAME} or any matter relating to {VICTIM_NAME}'s enrollment, attendance, conduct, or safety
- All correspondence between district employees discussing {VICTIM_NAME}, this family, or any complaint or concern raised by this family
- All communications between the district and any external party (legal counsel, insurance carrier, risk pool administrator, state agency, federal agency) relating to {VICTIM_NAME} or this family

**Policies and Procedures:**
- All policies, procedures, regulations, and administrative directives in effect at any time relevant to the matters described herein
- All training materials relating to discrimination, harassment, retaliation, tribal enrollment, ICWA, or special education

**Electronic Data:**
- All metadata associated with any document produced or maintained in electronic form, including creation dates, modification dates, author information, and version history
- All audit logs for the student information system showing who accessed or modified records relating to {VICTIM_NAME}, and when

---

## II. PROHIBITED CONDUCT

Effective immediately upon receipt of this letter, {SCHOOL_DISTRICT_NAME} and all personnel are prohibited from:

1. Destroying, deleting, discarding, overwriting, or otherwise disposing of any record within the scope described above
2. Altering, modifying, amending, or supplementing any existing record without preserving the original version
3. Converting text-searchable electronic records to image-only format (e.g., printing and re-scanning documents)
4. Permitting any automatic document destruction, retention schedule, or data deletion routine to apply to records within this scope
5. Removing documents from regular storage or relocating them in a manner that would impede retrieval

---

## III. LEGAL BASIS AND CONSEQUENCES OF NON-COMPLIANCE

This preservation demand is issued pursuant to {COMPLAINANT_NAME}'s right to pursue civil rights claims under Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. § 2000d), 42 U.S.C. § 1983, FERPA (20 U.S.C. § 1232g), and applicable state law.

The destruction, alteration, or degradation of records following receipt of this notice constitutes:

- Spoliation of evidence, which courts may address through adverse inference instructions, dismissal of defenses, or sanctions
- A potential violation of {STATE_RECORDS_RETENTION_STATUTE} ({STATE})
- A potential violation of 18 U.S.C. § 1519 (destruction of records in a federal investigation) if any federal investigation is pending or reasonably anticipated
- A potential violation of 18 U.S.C. § 1512(c) (obstruction of official proceedings)

Courts have held that a litigation hold obligation arises when litigation is "reasonably anticipated" — a standard met when a party receives a records preservation demand of this nature. *Zubulake v. UBS Warburg LLC*, 220 F.R.D. 212, 218 (S.D.N.Y. 2003).
